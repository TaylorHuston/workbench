# Practice

Original projects that are usually intended to practice something specific.&#x20;

As we hand more and more of our coding off to LLMs our own coding skills will atrophe. Regularly coding things from scratch, even if they're simple, should be an effective way to help prevent that. And when learning something new, coding or not, the best way is to practice it. Not just memorize it. Similar to how restating something in your own words, even going so far as to "teach" it to someone else, is much more effective for your own learning than just doing a bunch of multiple choice quizzes or something.

Similarly, tutorials have their place, but if you just blindly follow one, essentially copying the code as you go, that isn't really a good way to learn something. Video (YouTube) tutorials are especially ineffective to me. I've tried to learn new things that way and it's honestly a painful experiment. You want to follow along and build what they're building, which sounds good in theory, but in practice it means you're just constantly pausing the video and trying to copy the code that they've written, scrubbing back and forth because now you're app isn't working like thiers is, so you're trying to figure out which part you missed or fat fingered. Or it just turns out that the video tutorial is too old so some part of what they're doing doesn't work anymore. At least with written tutorials or books that is less of an issue, a book doesn't keep reading itself when you zone out and written tutorials can be updated easily. But at the end, you're still just copying, not thinking.

Some of the projects in here will be completely original. Many will probably be based on a tutorial. But the plan is to treat it more like school. I will watch the tutorial, take notes like it's a lecture, then build a thing from scratch from those notes. Depending on the project I might try to do my best to duplicate it completely or it might be something that's just loosely inspired by it.&#x20;
